## Project Overview
Collaborated with the **HERA (High-Altitude Engineering for Research in Astrophysics)** team alongside Drexel University and the Pennsylvania Space Grant Consortium (PSGC). Contributed to high-altitude balloon equipment design and engineered ruggedized tungsten payload mounting stacks capable of surviving extreme 10G shock loads.

## Key Engineering Features
- **High-G Shock Load Design:** Engineered and analyzed structural mounting assemblies to withstand severe 10G shock forces encountered during balloon launch and parachute deployment cycles.
- **Dense Material Integration:** Utilized tungsten components within strict weight and spatial constraints to optimize radiation/mass shielding and balance payload distribution.
- **Research Collaboration:** Co-authored technical abstracts and documentation detailing mechanical mounting stack specifications for high-altitude flight readiness.

## Summary
Co-authored research abstracts and designed tungsten structural mounting stacks to withstand 10G shock loads for high-altitude balloon instrumentation under the HERA (High-Altitude Engineering for Research in Astrophysics) collaboration alongside Drexel University and NASA PSGC. This project involved rigorous mechanical validation, finite element analysis, and material selection to ensure payload integrity under extreme atmospheric flight conditions. The resulting hardware successfully met all structural compliance targets, bridging theoretical aerospace concepts with hands-on hardware fabrication and testing.
