## Project Overview
Worked with the **HERA (High-Altitude Engineering for Research in Astrophysics)** team at Drexel University and the Pennsylvania Space Grant Consortium. Created high-altitude balloon equipment design and engineered tungsten payload mounting stacks capable of surviving extreme 10G shock loads.

## Key Engineering Features
- **High-G Shock Load Design:** Engineered and analyzed (FMEA) mounting assemblies to survive 10G vertical and horizontal shock forces encountered during balloon launch and termination (parachute deployment).
- **Dense Material Integration:** Utilized tungsten container parts within strict weight and spatial constraints to optimize radiation/mass shielding and balanced payload weight distribution.
- **Research Collaboration:** Co-authored technical abstracts and documentation about mechanical mounting stacks for high-altitude flight feasibility and for research grants.

## Summary
Co-authored research abstracts and designed tungsten containers and mounting stacks to survive 10G shock loads for high-altitude balloon payloads under HERA alongside Drexel University and NASA PSGC. This project involved high-level mechanical validation, multiple finite element analyses, and specific material selection to ensure payload durability under extreme atmospheric flight conditions (thermal, physical, particle factors). The resulting hardware successfully met all structural compliance targets, bridging theoretical aerospace concepts with hands-on hardware fabrication and testing.
